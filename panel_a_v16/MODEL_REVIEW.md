# Shared SSG and pillar section with exposed solid faces

The human pointed out that the exposed pillar section should not be covered by a foreground layer of SSG. The previous section stopped at y=0, in front of the idealized focus pair whose front edges began near y=0.83 mm. Its cloudy liquid layer therefore veiled the pillars instead of showing their bare section.

![Exposed pillar faces on a common section](03_pillar_context_v16_preview_white.png)

[PNG close-up asset](03_pillar_context_v16_asset.png)

This revision advances a single common display section through both focus pillars and the surrounding SSG to y=1.70 mm. The base, support, rim, cover and neighboring array share the same non-destructive cut. The resulting pillar faces use their own opaque warm-gray material and are directly exposed to the camera. The turbid blue-cyan SSG remains between and behind the solid sections. No material transparency mask is used to fake exposure of the pillar faces.

The camera advances by the same amount to keep the established framing. Source meshes, shape keys, gentle illustrative 0.25 mm midpoint bow and the v15 cloudy SSG shader settings remain unchanged. The cutting plane is a display choice for this idealized mechanism view, not a new fabrication dimension or a lowered liquid level. The enclosed structure remains filled to its cover outside the display cut.

16 checks passed after reopening the native file. Both visible pillar section caps exist on the same plane as the SSG section. For camera rays aimed at the middle of each exposed pillar face, the solid is hit before any SSG. All 30 sampled locations immediately behind the shared plane are occupied by either silicone or SSG, with no overlap at those samples. Native source mesh geometry and SSG material settings match v15; the local view remains free of arrows, leaders and text.

No physical simulation, Pro inquiry or local Git write was performed. Original DOCX/PPTX hashes remain unchanged. The section is an illustration of the enclosed SSG-pillar interaction, not a computed stress or fluid-flow result.
